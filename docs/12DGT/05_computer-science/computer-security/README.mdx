## Videos and diagrams

Each session links two or three YouTube videos with a question to guide viewing. The named **core viewing window** fits inside the lesson timetable — stop when it ends. Everything else is optional, not extra compulsory homework. Videos are supplements: the notes contain what you need for the activities even if YouTube is blocked at school.

<Callout kind="note">

Video titles and links were checked against web-indexed video pages or publisher references on 8 September 2026; playback and regional/school availability were not tested. Some videos are older — use the written notes for current password and cryptography guidance, and don't memorise historical statistics from them. Review sessions deliberately reuse earlier videos with new questions.

Diagrams are included as editable **Mermaid** code blocks. A Markdown viewer with Mermaid support renders them as diagrams; each also has a plain-language explanation, so the notes still work in a basic text editor.

</Callout>

## Our continuing case study

<Callout kind="case">

**The Harbour School Robotics Club** has 80 members, two student treasurers and a teacher supervisor. It stores contact details and payment records. Its laptop is sometimes taken home. Right now, members share one password, everyone can edit the records, and the only copy lives on that one laptop. Your job, across this unit, is to improve that arrangement — without making the club impossible to actually run.

Use fictional information and disposable files for practical work. Do not try attacks on real accounts or networks. Never put a real password into a course answer or an online password checker.

</Callout>
