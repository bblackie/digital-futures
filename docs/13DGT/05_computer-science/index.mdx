# Unit 5: Computer Science

This unit prepares you for **AS91908 – Analyse an area of computer science**, a 3-credit external assessment. It is an **online digital examination** with a printed resource booklet, and you write short and/or extended answers totalling **800–1500 words** on **ONE** area you choose in the exam room.

---

## Which area does this cover?

The 2026 specification lists three areas you can choose between on the day:

- **Complexity and tractability**
- **Big data**
- **Formal languages**

**This unit currently documents Big Data only.** That is the area covered on this page and the topics below. Complexity/tractability and formal languages are valid exam choices but are not yet written up here — ask your teacher for separate material if you plan to answer one of those instead.

:::info Don't confuse this with AS91909
The supplied `91909-spc-2026.pdf` specification document covers **both** 91908 and 91909 on the same pages. AS91909 ("Present a reflective analysis of developing a digital outcome") is a **different standard** about your own project work — it is not part of this unit and is not covered here.
:::

## Big Data: what's assessed

Per the specification, Big Data questions may cover:

- **Characteristics** of big data (volume, variety, velocity, etc.)
- **Generation, processing and analysing** data in different formats
- **Interpretation and representation** (bias and display)
- **Tools and technologies** used in big data
- **Considerations**: privacy, ethics, and data governance

You'll also be expected to apply this understanding to an **unfamiliar context** in the exam, and to spot where big data overlaps with other computer science areas — most often **artificial intelligence**.

## How to use these notes

Each topic explains a mechanism or concept, then works through a **past-paper-style question** so you can see how the ideas are actually examined. NZQA hasn't released marking schedules for these papers, so treat the worked examples as guidance on structure and depth — not a guaranteed mark scheme.

A strong 91908 answer usually does three things:

1. **Explains a mechanism accurately** — in your own words, using the correct vocabulary
2. **Connects it to a specific effect on people** — not "society" in the abstract, but a named group
3. **Reaches a justified conclusion or comparison** — especially for Merit/Excellence-level questions ("discuss", "critically analyse")
